/ LITERARY FICTION

The Salt Between Us — a diaspora novel, built from Lagos to London.

Debut novelZero platform14-week build

Adaeze's debut follows three generations of a Nigerian-British family across the Atlantic. Her publisher had given her a stock Amazon ad template and a prayer. We built a landing page around a free chapter — "The Crossing," the 1962 migration scene — and a glossary of Igbo proverbs woven through the text.

The targeting was specific: readers of Chimamanda, Ayọ̀bámi Adébáyọ̀, and the "African diaspora literary fiction" shelf on Goodreads. Broad "literary fiction" audiences were tested for one week and killed — four times more expensive, half the opt-in rate.

Miriam Halter2023 · Memoir
/ MEMOIR

Bone Cartography — a memoir of place, mapped in letters.

Second bookSmall press10-week build

Miriam's memoir traces her grandmother's displacement from a Polish village that no longer appears on modern maps. The magnet we built was a "field notebook" — 22 pages of her grandmother's handwritten place names, translated, with Miriam's marginalia alongside.

The targeting was unusual: we aimed at readers of W.G. Sebald, Robert Macfarlane, and the "nature writing / place memoir" shelf. The opt-in rate surprised even us — 44%, the highest in the archive. Readers of place-based non-fiction, it turns out, are unusually willing to trade an email for a real artifact.

Vikram Desai2025 · Mythic fantasy
/ MYTHIC FANTASY

Monsoon Gods — a pantheon, given freely.

Debut novelSelf-published12-week build

Vikram's debut reimagines the Hindu monsoon deities as a family in crisis. He had no platform, no mailing list, and a deep suspicion of "marketing." We built a glossary of forty deities, rivers, and rites — typeset to match the novel's design — and a short story about the youngest god, the one the novel is named after.

The targeting was the most culturally specific we've run: "South Asian mythic fantasy," "Hindu speculative fiction," "folklore retellings." We explicitly excluded broad "fantasy" audiences after a one-week test showed them at $4.87 per reader. The final blended cost: $1.12.

Siobhan Byrne2024 · Literary horror
/ LITERARY HORROR

The Drowning House — a folk tale, a field recording, a list.

Third bookMid-size press8-week build

Siobhan's novel is set in a flooded Victorian house on the Irish coast, and the horror is inherited — the kind that lives in family stories. The magnet we built was a transcript of a "field recording" — a fictional oral history of the house, written as if recorded in 1974 by a folklore student.

The targeting was the most fun we've had: readers of Sarah Perry, Susan Hill, and the "Irish gothic" shelf. We also ran a small test against "cozy mystery" readers — it performed terribly, which told us something useful about the difference between atmospheric and cozy.

/ DIASPORA SF

Quiet Machines — a future, built from the border.

Debut novelSmall press11-week build

Tomás's debut is set in a near-future Tijuana where the border wall has become a computational surface. The magnet was a "technical manual" — a fictional document from the novel's world, explaining how the wall's sensors work, written in the voice of the novel's engineer protagonist.

The targeting was the most niche we've attempted: "Latinx science fiction," "border literature," "speculative fiction by authors of color." The audience was small but fiercely engaged — 52% open rate on the welcome letter, the highest in the archive.

/ CULINARY MEMOIR

Honey & Ash — a recipe, a family, a list.

Second bookSelf-published9-week build

Leila's memoir traces her family's displacement from Tehran through the recipes they carried with them. The magnet was a single recipe — "Fesenjān for Exiles" — tested, photographed, and typeset to match the book's design. It took three weeks to get the pomegranate molasses ratio right.

// 04 · A NOTE ON THE WORK

Every book is different. The system is the same.

The magnet changes shape — a recipe, a field recording, a technical manual, a proverb glossary. The targeting changes — different shelves, different audiences, different costs. The letters change — different voices, different rhythms, different invitations.

But the system is always the same: a page you own, a gift worth keeping, modest ads aimed at the right shelf, and letters that treat the reader like an adult. That's it. That's the whole strategy.

"The machine is quiet. The machine is mine. The machine works."— Siobhan Byrne, September 2024

Every engagement in this archive began with a single email. No calls. No pitches. No "let's hop on a quick call to discuss." Just a book, a story, and a written proposal within two working days.

What every engagement includes

  • A landing page you own, exportable on day one
  • A lead magnet worth keeping — typeset to match the book
  • Modest ad budgets, reviewed in writing every month
  • A welcome sequence written in the book's voice
  • The Signal Report — plain words on what's working
  • Full list handover, always, for any reason
  • Zero calls. Ever. Unless you ask (and even then, we'd rather write)
